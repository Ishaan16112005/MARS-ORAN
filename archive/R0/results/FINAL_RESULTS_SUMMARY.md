# MARS-O-RAN Final Results Summary

## Experimental basis

The included results are generated by the Python digital-twin prototype using 4 policies, 30 deterministic seeds (20-49), a 300-s horizon per run, 100 nominal PRBs, one cell, one edge server, four slices, and a five-task emergency mission. All numbers are synthetic simulation outputs.

## Main result

| Policy | Mission success | Deadline success | Mean control latency (ms) | P95 E2E (ms) | Reconfig. cost | Mission loss | VaR90 | CVaR90 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| STATIC | 0.187 | 0.200 | 18.93 | 28.63 | 14.0 | 45.16 | 44.98 | 45.16 |
| PROPORTIONAL | 0.193 | 0.193 | 22.21 | 28.78 | 3413.8 | 45.12 | 44.98 | 45.12 |
| MISSION_AWARE | 0.727 | 1.000 | 21.32 | 28.63 | 1828.5 | 18.07 | 21.48 | 24.54 |
| MARS_RISK_AWARE | 0.707 | 1.000 | 18.45 | 28.63 | 1029.7 | 18.64 | 21.98 | 23.26 |

For the selected parameterization, MARS-RISK-AWARE changes mean mission success from 0.187 to 0.707 versus STATIC and reduces empirical CVaR90 mission loss by 48.5% (45.16 -> 23.26). Relative to the MISSION-AWARE baseline, it reduces mean reconfiguration cost by 43.7% (1828.5 -> 1029.7) and mean control latency by 13.4% (21.32 -> 18.45 ms), while mean mission success is 0.707 versus 0.727. This is a parameterized risk/stability trade-off, not a universal dominance claim.

## Core calculations used by the simulator

1. **Radio service rate:** `R_s = b_s W_PRB log2(1 + gamma_s) eta FBL_s h_s`
2. **Transmission time:** `T_tx = L/R`
3. **Edge service time:** `T_cmp = C/f`
4. **End-to-end delay proxy:** `T_E2E = T_queue + T_tx + T_xhaul + T_cmp + T_feedback`
5. **Dynamic criticality:** `chi_k = w_k [1 + alpha_u U_k + alpha_h H_k + alpha_cp I_cp + alpha_a A_k + alpha_r R_k]`
6. **AoI:** reset to 1 s after a fresh update, otherwise increment by 1 s.
7. **Reliability:** `P_succ = P_radio * P_edge * P_backhaul`
8. **Reconfiguration:** `C_rec(t) = sum_s |b_s(t)-b_s(t-1)|`
9. **Mission loss:** `L = sum_k chi_k(1-q_k)`
10. **Tail risk:** empirical VaR90/CVaR90 of mission loss over Monte Carlo runs.

## Algorithmic novelty implemented

The project contribution is the **combined mission-aware, risk-constrained control abstraction**: mission-DAG readiness determines which task matters now; criticality combines urgency, hazard, critical-path position, AoI pressure and risk; PRB allocation is coupled to edge service; task success requires deadline + freshness + reliability; CVaR measures tail mission loss; and hysteresis/step limits constrain reconfiguration instability.

## Ablation finding

The current ablation makes hysteresis the clearest stability component: removing hysteresis raises average reconfiguration cost from about 1091 to 2805 PRB-time units and lowers mean mission success from 0.767 to 0.633 in the ablation seeds. AoI and risk-reserve components have weaker marginal impact in this particular single-cell configuration, so the paper treats that as a research limitation and motivates multi-cell/mobility validation.

## What is completed vs. what is next

Completed through Phase 10: scenario specification, mission simulator, radio/MEC model, baselines, dynamic criticality, elastic slicing, AoI/reliability/stability control, CVaR evaluation, Monte Carlo, sensitivity, ablation, live browser dashboard, figures, technical report and LaTeX manuscript.

Still required for a higher-fidelity publishable validation: packet-level ns-3/5G-LENA or equivalent, multi-cell interference and mobility, calibrated traffic/channel traces, real O-RAN E2/A1 integration, and a solver/RL comparison.
